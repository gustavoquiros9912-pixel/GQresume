MetLife

Client Self-Service App & International Web Portals

MetLife, a global leader in insurance and financial services, needed to modernize how customers and agents manage policies across several markets. I led design for the Client Self-Service app (CSS) and Client Web Portal (CWP) in Mexico, and the Agent Web Portal (AWP) in the United States and Colombia.

As UX/CX Lead, my focus was making insurance understandable: turning dense policy, payment and claims information into screens people could act on, and building one design system that could flex across products, platforms and markets.

01 — the challenge

Insurance people can actually understand

  • Dense information. Coverage, premiums and policy status were written in insurance terms most customers don’t use.
  • High-stakes tasks. Payments, claims and medical processes leave little room for confusion.
  • Disconnected experiences. The app and the web portals had to feel like one MetLife without losing what each market needed locally.
  • Room to grow. Whatever we built had to support new products and markets without starting over.

The goal: make insurance clear, approachable and consistent on every platform and in every market.

02 — my role

03 — approach

Three decisions that shaped the work

The product was already full of information. The job was deciding what to show first, how to explain it and how to keep it consistent.

01

Explain it where it appears

Progressive disclosure

Decision. Instead of sending people to a glossary or an agent, each policy screen leads with the essentials and puts plain-language help right next to the term, through tooltips and status tags.

Example. “Premium” comes with a one-line explanation, and a policy that’s no longer active says what that means and what to do next.

02

One system, local flows

Scalable systems

Decision. I built a shared foundation of named color tokens, a two-typeface type scale and components with defined default, hover, focus and error states. Each product and market built its own flows on top of it.

Result. The app and portals look and behave like one MetLife, while each market keeps the flows it needs.

03

Calm the high-stakes moments

Empathy through clarity

Decision. Payments, claims and medical processes are broken into steps, explained before they start and closed with a clear confirmation.

Detail. Error states are part of the system, with their own colors and inline messages, so problems are flagged gently and the fix is shown right there.

05 — reflection

What I took from it

  • Clarity is a design decision. In insurance, the most useful screen is often the one that explains a single word well.
  • Systems earn trust across markets. Shared tokens and components let each market move on its own without the brand drifting.
  • Regulated doesn’t have to mean rigid. Working closely with legal and quality teams made it possible to meet strict requirements and still write for people.
