BJC Health

Internal Web Platform & Website Design

Through the agency Hedy & Hopp, I was Lead Designer on two streams of work for BJC, the St. Louis–based health system. The first was a confidential internal platform for the BJC HealthCare / Washington University School of Medicine Healthcare Innovation Lab. The second was design for BJC’s public website.

On the platform, I defined the structure and the user flows for each staff role. On the website, I designed new pages and templates and kept every update consistent, accessible and on brand.

01 — the challenge

Confidential on the inside, consistent on the outside

  • Sensitive by default. The Innovation Lab platform was for internal use only, so who could see and do what had to be built into the structure, not added later.
  • Many roles, many workflows. Different staff roles needed different paths through the same platform without getting lost.
  • A public site that keeps changing. New pages and regular updates put brand consistency and accessibility at risk.

The goal: secure, clear tools for staff and a reliable, consistent website for the public.

02 — my role

Lead designer on both streams

I led design of the internal platform and designed for the public website, working with BJC’s IT and compliance teams.

  • Defined the information architecture for the internal platform
  • Designed user flows for multiple staff roles
  • Designed responsive web interfaces
  • Designed new website pages and templates and updated existing ones
  • Kept the website’s components and style guide consistent
  • Reviewed pages for accessibility and brand consistency

03 — approach

Three decisions that shaped the work

Two very different audiences, one principle: make the right path obvious, whether you’re a staff member on an internal tool or a patient on the public site.

01

Design permissions into the structure

Security-first design

Decision. I designed the flows around each staff role, with access control and clear permissions built into the platform’s architecture.

Why. Building permissions into the structure protects sensitive work without adding steps for the people using it.

02

Fewer steps, clearer paths

Clarity in navigation

Decision. I simplified navigation and reduced redundancy, so each role had a clearer path through its tasks.

Why. An internal tool should be quick to learn and easy to come back to.

03

A system for the public site

Unified standards

Decision. New pages started from templates and shared components backed by a style guide, and pages were checked for accessibility and brand consistency as part of QA.

Why. On a site that’s always changing, consistency only lasts if it’s built into how the work gets done.

04 — impact

What was delivered

  • A confidential internal platform for the Healthcare Innovation Lab, with permissions designed into its structure
  • Role-based user flows with simplified navigation and reduced redundancy
  • New public website pages and templates built on shared components
  • Website updates checked for accessibility and brand consistency

Platform details are confidential due to project sensitivity.

05 — reflection

What I took from it

  • Privacy is an architecture decision. Defining roles and permissions early keeps a platform both safe and simple.
  • Internal tools deserve the same care as public ones. Staff time is valuable, and clear flows respect it.
  • Consistency is a process, not a one-time pass. Templates, a style guide and a QA step are what keep a changing site on brand and accessible.
