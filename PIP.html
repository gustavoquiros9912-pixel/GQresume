PIP Global Safety

Global Product Migration & Rebranding

When PIP Global Safety acquired Honeywell’s safety division, every product, label, manual and web page carrying the Honeywell name had to become PIP. That meant [~X,000] assets in one year, and every one had to stay compliant in every market where it was sold.

As Lead UI/UX & Product Designer, I started with the Americas portfolio and later expanded to Europe and Asia. My job was less about designing assets one at a time and more about building the system, priorities and review process that let the team move quickly while staying compliant.

01 — the challenge

A rebrand where mistakes are a safety problem

  • Volume against a fixed date. [~X,000] assets across [X] product lines had to switch brands within a year, while products kept shipping.
  • Regulated content. This is protective equipment. Certification marks, warnings and usage instructions differ by region and can’t be lost or altered when the brand changes.
  • Many hands. Regional teams, product owners and compliance reviewers all touched the files. Without a shared system, the new brand would have splintered into dozens of local versions.

On a normal rebrand, a wrong label is a brand mistake. Here it could become a compliance failure.

03 — approach

Three decisions that made it manageable

At this volume, redesigning items one by one wasn’t possible. The real work was deciding what order to go in, what to standardize and what to leave flexible.

01

Triage before design

Operational clarity

Decision. Instead of working through the catalog in order, I ranked assets by [sales volume and regulatory deadlines] and grouped them into [X] waves, so the products customers saw most moved first.

Trade-off. Low-volume lines waited for later waves, which I agreed on with product leads up front rather than leaving it to chance.

02

Templates, not one-offs

Design systems discipline

Decision. I built modular templates with locked brand zones (logo, color, type, layout grid) and flexible content zones for product data and regulatory text.

Result. [X] templates covered [~X%] of the portfolio, so most assets became a fill-in task instead of a design task.

03

Localize the content, not the brand

Global adaptability

Decision. Regional teams owned certifications, languages and warnings. The brand layer stayed fixed worldwide, and compliance reviewed each wave with me.

Result. Europe and Asia were rolled out on the same template system, with regional content adapted on top of it.

05 — reflection

What I’d take forward

  • Design systems aren’t only for screens. Locked zones, flexible content and clear ownership worked on packaging and manuals just as well as on a component library.
  • At scale, decisions are the bottleneck. Settling the order and the rules early mattered more than any single design choice.
  • Bring compliance in early. Making regional compliance a partner at each wave, rather than a final sign-off, caught problems while they were still cheap to fix.
